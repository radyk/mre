    No contracted answer covers that one, so this is me reading the evidence directly — each line below says what backs it.

    An optimality gap is the distance between the best solution the solver has actually found (its incumbent) and the best lower bound it has proven no solution can beat — it is reported as a percentage of that bound, and it means 'the true optimum is somewhere between the incumbent's cost and (1 minus the gap) times it, but we stopped searching before pinning it down further'.  [general knowledge — how scheduling works in general, not a fact about this plan]
    A gap of 0% means the solver has proven the incumbent is optimal; a gap of, say, 5% means the incumbent could be up to 5% worse than the true best possible schedule, but the solver ran out of time or hit a stopping rule before closing the gap further — it does not mean the schedule is 5% wrong in any specific place, only that nothing better than the bound has been ruled out.  [general knowledge — how scheduling works in general, not a fact about this plan]
    The lower bound side of the gap is often weak for scheduling objectives like tardiness or makespan, because relaxations (dropping integrality or precedence) can be far looser than the real problem — so a stubbornly large reported gap can mean the bound is poor rather than that the incumbent schedule is actually far from optimal.  [general knowledge — how scheduling works in general, not a fact about this plan]
