    An optimality gap is the percentage (or absolute) difference between the best solution found so far and the best proven lower bound on the objective — it tells you how far the current answer could still be from the true optimum, not how far it is.  [general knowledge — how scheduling works in general, not a fact about this plan]
    A gap of 0% means the solution is proven optimal; a gap of, say, 5% means the true optimum is somewhere between the current solution and a point 5% better, but the solver has not searched enough to close that distance further.  [general knowledge — how scheduling works in general, not a fact about this plan]
    The gap shrinks in two ways: the found solution gets better (the upper bound tightens) or the proof gets stronger (the lower bound tightens) — a gap can stay wide even with a perfectly good schedule, simply because the bound is weak, especially for tardiness or scheduling objectives which tend to give loose bounds.  [general knowledge — how scheduling works in general, not a fact about this plan]
