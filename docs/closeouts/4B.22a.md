## 2. The trade, stated plainly

**There is no dense-and-proved option, and the reason is not what 4B.12 would
have predicted.**

* **The 14-day window -- the shipped one -- does not survive this density at all.**
  90 orders solve with an 81.9% gap; 140 and 170 return **UNKNOWN**, meaning no
  solution at all and an **empty board**; 360 is proven **INFEASIBLE** in 2.8
  seconds. Between the current 40-order board and 140 orders, the shipped window
  goes from a proved optimum to nothing on screen.
* **Shorter windows buy solvability, not proof.** Every populated demo candidate
  came back FEASIBLE with a gap between **76.5% and 92.4%**. Not one proved.
* **The past-due work is FREE, and this was the measurement worth taking.**
  4B.12 says R-PD1's admitted past-due work is what makes tardiness nonzero at
  the lightest density and destroys the proof. So `c11-w7-280-nopd` is `c7` with
  `pd_share = 0.0` and nothing else changed. It is **still FEASIBLE, gap 84.5%**
  -- 2.5 points better than the same board WITH 47 past-due orders on it. Density
  alone loses the proof here. Dropping R-PD1's entire on-board specimen buys
  almost nothing, so it was kept.

So the choice is not *proved-and-empty* against *full-and-unproved-because-of-
past-due*. It is:

**RECOMMENDATION: take `c9-w10-280`.** Reasons, in order:

1. **A DISPLACEMENT THAT COSTS NOTHING IS NOT A NEGOTIATION.** The sandbox is
   the feature the whole negotiation-cockpit thesis rests on. On the current
   board the same gesture IS priced ($354.58) -- but every cent is the moved
   order's own tardiness, and the order it shoves three days later pays
   **$0.00**, because nothing downstream is tight enough to care. A long
   backward drag on that board prices at exactly **$0.00** with three affected
   orders and not a dollar between them. On the new board the same gesture costs
   **$2,596.67**, split **$0.00 window re-optimization / $2,596.67 your move**,
   and **$2,301.67 of it is paid by the order that got pushed**. That is the
   conversation the card was built to have (section 5(e)).
2. **The proof was not ours to keep.** No candidate that fills the machines
   proves, and the controlled pair shows it is density, not past-due work. The
   only proved board available is the empty one.
3. **The unproved gap RENDERS CORRECTLY and leads the opener** -- item 1 of six,
   in money, with the bound stated as an upper limit rather than a saving
   (section 5). 4B.11 built exactly this and it has never had a board to say it
   on.
4. **Four capabilities get a specimen for the first time**: R-PD1's past-due
   scheduling (47 orders), the tardiness floor/controllable split (a real
   $535,800 / $1,504,346.67), a coarse zone that genuinely BINDS (8 cells at
   95-99% of derated capacity), and a beyond-horizon tray with 122 orders behind
   it.
5. **10 days is a measured choice, not a compromise.** 14 gives an empty board
   at this density and 7 gives fewer bars (286) at a similar gap. `window_days`
   is a per-solve request field, so this is a solve parameter, not a product
   change.

**What it costs, said plainly:** a stranger's first glance now includes
*"the cost optimum is NOT proved -- a gap of 92.4%"* and *"96 orders finish
late"*. Both are true, both are rendered in the product's own honest register,
and neither can be avoided while filling the machines. If Daryn would rather
demo a proved board, `rolling-c362baa4-1b0` is still registered and still says
*optimum proved* -- that is what makes this a choice rather than a fait accompli.

movable bar onto its FIRST bar's start -- a six-week backward jump. On the old
board that priced at exactly $0.00:**

```
  outcome              verdict          status  OPTIMAL
  cost_delta_abs       0.0             attribution      split
  reopt_delta_abs      0.0             move_delta_abs   0.0
  baseline_total_cost  16481.95        lateness_delta_min  0
  affected_orders      3 rows - EVERY tardiness_delta 0.0
```

**The same gesture on the NEW board returned INFEASIBLE** -- *"pin infeasible this
horizon"* -- because the operation's own predecessor had not finished. That is a
correct answer to a question no planner would ask, and it is why the picker was
rewritten to short forward shifts. **So the corrected gesture was re-run on the
old board too**, and it does NOT price at zero:

```
  outcome              verdict          status  OPTIMAL   message "optimal delta proven"
  cost_delta_abs       354.58          attribution      split
  reopt_delta_abs      0.0            move_delta_abs   354.58
  baseline_total_cost  16481.95       lateness_delta_min  851
  moves                ORD-000013 op20  +1440 min (pinned)
                       ORD-000022       +4320 min
  affected_orders      ORD-000013  tardiness_delta 354.58  lateness_delta_min 1439
                       ORD-000022  tardiness_delta   0.00  lateness_delta_min 4320
  cost_lines           tardiness 354.58; setup 0.0; production 0.0/0.0; other 0.0
```

**The honest comparison is therefore this, and it is sharper than "$0 versus
something".** On both boards a short forward collision is feasible and priced.
What differs is **whether displacing something costs anything**:

| | `rolling-c362baa4-1b0` | `rolling-c9973708-865` |
|---|---|---|
| the move costs | $354.58 | **$2,596.67** |
| paid by the moved order | $354.58 | $295.00 |
| **paid by the order it displaced** | **$0.00**, pushed 4,320 min for free | **$2,301.67**, pushed 5,524 min |
| proof of the delta | OPTIMAL, proven | FEASIBLE, ~ delta, bound not proven |

On the empty board the displaced order absorbs a three-day push at no cost
because nothing downstream is tight; the card can only ever show the planner's
own order paying. **On the dense board displacement has a price and the card
names who pays it** -- which is the conversation the sandbox exists to have. The
earlier `$0.00` figure is real, but it belongs to a gesture the new board
correctly refuses, so it is reported here rather than used as the headline.

## 6. What changed, and why none of it is a product change

**`tools/generate_erp_dataset.py`** -- one new preset, `demo_board`, and three
optional parameters on `_apply_pilot_scale` that only it uses. The generator is
a tool; the demo world is a fixture. No module under `src/mre/` was touched, so
nothing about how the product schedules, prices or explains anything moved.

`demo_board` = pilot_scale's authored physics (15 machines in capability groups,
the splittable P-SPACER, the PAINT changeover matrix, the second-Wednesday
maintenance closure, the Saturday HEAT overtime window, declared coarse-horizon
coefficients) with exactly three differences: **280 orders**, **12% of the book
already past due**, and a **due-date median pulled in to 10 days** from
pilot_scale's deliberately wide look-ahead spread.

**`pilot_scale` IS BYTE-IDENTICAL and that is checked, not claimed.**
`prove_pilot_scale_unchanged.py` regenerates it under the new code and diffs
every file the solver reads against a baseline generated from `HEAD`, at 40
orders and at 400. The risk was never the emitted values -- it was the RANDOM
STREAM. An unconditional `rng.random()` for the past-due draw would have shifted
every later draw in the order loop and emitted a different book from the same
seed with every line of authored physics untouched. `past_due_share > 0 and ...`
short-circuits, and the two other knobs change nothing at their defaults; the
script is what makes that a measurement.

**A past-due order here has `created_date` BEFORE its due date.** `facility_real`
writes `created = ref` unconditionally and so emits an inverted pair the M0 gate
flags -- deliberately left in place as the live `proceeded_flagged` specimen
(docs/07 5a.30), and deliberately **not** reproduced here. This board's
past-due work is a true statement about the plant, so it must not wear a
data-defect finding: R-PD1 clause (2) exactly. The board grades **ACCEPTED**.

**`splittable_weight` was measured and REJECTED.** The knob remains so the
result can be reproduced; the preset declares no value. See section 7(d).

**`tools/spikes/demo_board_4b22a/`** -- seven scripts and a README. Nothing in
`tests/` changed: `demo_board` carries `feel=True`, so the three
scenario-enumerating test modules skip it exactly as they skip `pilot_scale` and
`facility_real`.

## 7. Findings -- named and left

**(a) THE CONCENTRATION BAND CANNOT FIRE AT ANY DENSITY THIS ERRAND COULD MINT,
AND DENSITY IS NOT WHY.** 4B.16 recorded that the band never fired on either
measured board and put it down to demo density. It is not density.
`Explainer._opener_load` divides run minutes by the machine's **whole resolved
calendar** -- 28,080 minutes on the new board, against a plan that occupies about
25 days -- so the busiest lane on a 386-bar board reads **39.7%**, against
`SATURATED = 0.85`. Measured on both boards, at the opener's own denominator:

| | busiest lane | its opener utilisation | denominator |
|---|---|---|---|
| `c0-current` | CUT-01, 5,981 run min | **26.8%** | 22,320 min |
| `c9-w10-280` | PAINT-01, 11,139 run min | **39.7%** | 28,080 min |

Raising density raises the numerator, but the finding needs a ratio against a
denominator fixed by the calendar rather than by the plan. `_opener_load` filters
at 50% before it even looks for alternatives, so on both boards it returns
nothing at all. This is 4B.20's class exactly -- a capacity figure whose
denominator is not the one the claim is about -- at a fifth site, and it is
REPORTED, not fixed. A second gate compounds it: concentration also requires an
eligible IDLE alternative, and on this plant only CUT and PRESS steps carry
alternates, so a saturated PAINT or MILL lane could never produce the finding
however hot it ran.

**(b) `order-schedule` DOES NOT VOICE THE PAST-DUE DISPOSITION.** R-PD1 clause
(6) says every per-order route voices the disposition. Asked *"where is
ORD-000040"*, the route lists four placements and stops -- about an order that was
due **2025-12-15**, twenty-one days before the plan's origin, finishes **34.8
days late**, and carries **$20,860.00** of tardiness of which **$12,000.00 is
floor**. Every one of those facts is in the document's own service outcome. The
planner is shown a tidy four-line itinerary for an order that is the single worst
thing on their board. This could not be seen before: no demo board has ever
carried a past-due order, which is the whole reason the errand put 47 of them
on one.

**(c) `what-would-change` OFFERED A START DATE THREE WEEKS BEFORE THE PLAN
EXISTS.** *"PRESS-FAST had open, unheld time from 2025-12-22 07:00, 864h before
it started"* -- on a board whose reference origin is **2026-01-05**. The scan
starts from the operation's upstream floor, and for a past-due order that floor
is its old release date. The arithmetic is right and the sentence is unusable:
nothing can be started in December. Also a first sighting, and for the same
reason as (b).

**(d) A DENSE BOARD CHUNKS FEWER OPERATIONS THAN AN EMPTY ONE, AND FORCING MORE
DESTROYS THE SOLVE.** The chosen board draws **1** chunked bar against the
current board's 2 -- the errand's one unmet target. The mechanism is measured,
not guessed: R-C3 chunking needs a splittable operation too long for one open
window, this plant has exactly one such product at ~4.5% of the book, and a long
splittable operation is the most expensive admission a window solve can make, so
most land in the tray where they draw nothing (at 280 orders, 1 of 9 placed).
Raising P-SPACER's weight 1 -> 4 (42 splittable ops) does not buy chunked bars:
it turns a FEASIBLE 386-bar board into **UNKNOWN -- an empty board -- at both the
7-day and the 10-day window**, 5.5 deterministic units spent for no solution
(c12, c13). Left as measured. 4B.20's per-chunk rendering therefore still has
only a single specimen on the demo path.

**(e) BEAT ONE SAYS "THIS PLACEMENT ISN'T POSSIBLE HERE" FROM A STATUS THAT
PROVED NOTHING.** The feasibility ghost returned `status: "UNKNOWN"`,
`feasible: false`, message *"this placement isn't possible here"* -- and beat two
then found the same pin FEASIBLE and priced it at $2,596.67. UNKNOWN means the
first-feasible solve could not tell inside its 2-second budget, which is a
different fact from impossible. The cockpit draws beat one's verdict first, so a
planner is told a move is impossible about a third of a second before being told
what it costs. R-T2 anticipates beat two contradicting beat one, but the
contradiction is supposed to be about PRICE. At 40 orders 2 seconds was enough
and this never showed; at 386 bars it is not.

**(f) THE 10-DAY WINDOW IS NON-MONOTONE IN SOLVABILITY.** 200 orders returns
UNKNOWN after 151.9 s; **280 orders on the same window returns FEASIBLE with 386
bars** after 161.4 s. More work, and the solver finds a solution where it found
none with less. This is 4B.12's shape ("the proof cost is not even MONOTONE")
appearing in SOLVABILITY rather than in proof cost, and it is the reason the
density was measured across a grid rather than bisected.

**(g) THE GATE SEES THE DENSITY.** The submission grades ACCEPTED with **four
data-quality findings, all `proceeded_flagged`**, and the opener's item 6 quotes
one of them: *"CUT-01 is in a workload too dense to schedule cleanly"*. A
finding about the plant's load arriving through the DATA-QUALITY channel is worth
a look -- it is not obviously wrong, but "too dense to schedule cleanly" is a
statement about capacity, and R-PD1 clause (2) says exclusion (and, by extension,
the defect vocabulary) is for data defects, never for a true statement about the
plant's position. Not investigated here.

**(h) THE AT-RISK BAND HAS ONE MEMBER ON A 386-BAR BOARD.** Item 3 of the opener
names ORD-000142 and nothing else. At-risk is conservative by construction
(4B.16: slack in calendar minutes against the longest step in WORKING minutes),
and with 96 orders already late there is little left in the on-time-but-tight
population. Not a defect; a limit, now measured at density for the first time.
