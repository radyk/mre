## 1. TURN 1 -- THE SPECIMEN, ANSWERED BACKWARDS SINCE THE COUNTERFACTUAL SHIPPED

This is the question, and this is what the demo board said to it at HEAD, an
hour before the fix. Verbatim, live ask path, `rolling-c9973708-865`:

    > can i move ORD-000057 later, maintenance wants the machine for the day
    [intent=what-would-change route=what-would-change conf=0.92]

    Answering about ORD-000057 op10 on CUT-03 - the first of its 4 operations.
    Nothing has to change for ORD-000057 op10 to start EARLIER: holding every
    other placement where it is, CUT-03 had open, unheld time from 2025-12-22
    07:00, 413h45m before it started. It was not prevented from going earlier -
    the solver preferred this placement.

Three sentences about moving the order forward, to a planner who asked to move
it out of the way. Every figure in them is true. Not one of them is about the
question.

And this is the same board, same question, after:

    > can i move ORD-000057 later, maintenance wants the machine for the day
    [intent=what-would-change route=what-would-change conf=0.95
     move_direction=LATER move_target='the day']

    Answering about ORD-000057 op10 on CUT-03 - the first of its 4 operations.
    Pushing ORD-000057 op10 out to Thursday 2026-01-15 07:00 - the first time
    CUT-03 is open after the planned maintenance it would be waiting on
    (2026-01-14 00:00 to 2026-01-14 23:59).

    Not there: a later start would run ORD-000057 straight into the next step of
    its own routing, which is already scheduled from 2026-01-09 07:00
    [docs/05 A1/A2].
    Offering you a still-later slot would make that worse, not better, so I am
    not offering one. What moves this is moving the downstream step too - a
    two-operation change I price one bar at a time and therefore cannot price as
    one gesture.

The answer is a REFUSAL, and that is the point of the session. 4B.27 declined to
half-build this precisely because on a real board a later move is usually a
refusal, so a version that handled only the priceable case would answer the easy
question and go quiet on the common one. The refusal above reads the maintenance
day the planner named, resolves the target after it, says which closure it read,
tests that instant, and then names the ONE constraint that actually stops it --
which turns out not to be the maintenance at all, but ORD-000057's own op20 on
MILL-01 the next morning.

## 3. ITEM 2 -- WHAT "LATER" MEANS, AND WHO IS ALLOWED TO DECIDE

`src/mre/modules/later_move.py`. Pure: planner words plus three calendar views
in, one disclosed target out.

**THE PARSE REPORTS; THE CALENDAR RESOLVES.** Two fields join `ParsedQuestion`:
`move_direction` (`earlier | later | unstated`, absent reading as earlier, so no
older parse becomes unanswerable) and `move_target` -- **the planner's own words,
verbatim and unresolved**. Which Friday, what time of day, whether the machine is
even open then are calendar questions, and 4B.15 Item 0 is the measured cost of
letting a model answer one: a true fact about the wrong Tuesday, in a horizon
with five of them. This is the `contested_claim` precedent (4B.14) applied to a
second field, and R-AI5(8) applied to a second decision.

**Three shapes, each disclosed:**

* **NAMED** -- "move it to Friday". Resolved to the nearest FUTURE instance past
  the current placement, and the answer says how many it chose between:
  *"There are 7 of that weekday still ahead of where it sits, and I took the
  first one. Name a date if you meant a different one."* An ISO date is the one
  unambiguous form and is taken as given.
* **MAGNITUDE** -- "push it out a week". Current start plus the amount, snapped
  FORWARD when that instant is not a working hour, **and the snap is stated**: a
  planner who asked for 20:00 and is quoted a price for 07:00 the next morning
  has to be told which one the number is about.
* **NEXT-FIT** -- bare "later". The first open, unheld stretch after the current
  placement with room for the WHOLE operation, computed by `earliest_fit`
  through the same R-C3 chunk discipline the blocker analysis uses.

**WHERE THE REASON NAMES AN INTERVAL, THE INTERVAL IS READ.** "maintenance wants
the machine for the day" is a target: the resolver finds the DECLARED closure
(docs/05 C2 -- an exception with a reason, never a gap in a window list), puts
the target after it, and the answer says which one it read, with its kind and its
span.

**THE RULE THIS SESSION HAD TO INVENT, AND IT MATTERS:**

> **A SHIFT BOUNDARY IS A SNAP; A DECLARED CLOSURE IS AN ANSWER.**

The first draft resolved a named day against the RESOLVED calendar -- closures
already subtracted -- which is what anyone would write and what every other
consumer of `_open_windows` correctly wants. Asked for the maintenance Wednesday
it returned the Thursday after, silently, and branch (b) became unreachable: no
target could ever land on a closure, so no answer could ever name one. A planner
asking about the maintenance day would have been handed a different day with
nothing on the surface mentioning maintenance.

So a named day and a magnitude resolve against the SHIFT PATTERN (closures NOT
subtracted); a closed day is reachable, a night is not; the target carries the
closure it landed in; and the pricer refuses it by name. Only the bare-"later"
fallback resolves against free time, because that shape asks where the operation
COULD go rather than testing a place. This is 4B.23's rule at another seam --
empty is not open, and shut-for-a-reason is not merely not-a-working-hour.

**A NAMED DAY THAT SITS LATER IS A LATER MOVE, AND ONLY THE CALENDAR CAN SAY
WHICH.** "can this move to Friday" carries no direction in its words; the parse
returns `unstated` and is right to. The decision is made in the assembler
(`_named_target_is_later`), where the dates are, on a computed fact -- never in
the prompt. It fails closed: anything it cannot resolve keeps the 4B.16
counterfactual, so every phrasing that reached it still reaches it.

## 4. ITEM 3 -- THE PRICEABLE ANSWER, AND WHY IT MAY STATE A FACT

Live, demo board, ORD-000208 op30 on PAINT-01 (a past-due order, last operation
of its routing):

    > what would pushing ORD-000208 out by a day cost

    Pushing ORD-000208 op30 out by "a day" puts it at Thursday 2026-01-22 16:49
    on PAINT-01.

    It costs +$600.00 (+0.028% of the plan's $2,127,482.58).
    Nothing else moves - every other job stays exactly where it is, which is the
    only reading under which this number is about YOUR move. The slot it leaves
    behind is simply unused in this price; a deeper search might refill it, and
    that is a different question with a different answer.

    ORD-000208 finishes 2026-01-22 19:00 - 19d 19h past its due date
    (2026-01-02 23:59). 2d of that was already sunk before this plan opened and
    no placement can move it [R-PD1]; 17d 19h is what this schedule decides,
    against 16d 19h today.
      Tardiness on this order: +$600.00 ($11,275.00 -> $11,875.00).

    Held-world price: every other placement pinned where it is, the whole ledger
    recomputed, and the result re-checked against the scheduler (OPTIMAL). It is
    what this move costs, not what the best plan containing it would cost.

**THIS ROUTE MAY SAY "IT COSTS" WHERE THE COUNTERFACTUAL MUST SAY "THAT REMOVES
THE BARRIER".** A lever is a necessary condition -- remove the bound and where
the operation lands still needs a re-solve, which R-AI4 forbids. A later move is
not a bound to relax: it is a PLACEMENT, and a placement can be PRICED. So the
number above is a measurement, not a hypothetical. The 4B.16
necessary-never-sufficient framing still governs anything said BEYOND the priced
pin, and only there.

Three things, every time, each from machinery that already existed:

* **WHAT THE DELAY COSTS** -- `local_price.price_local_move` (4B.24), the pin
  applied, the whole ledger recomputed, the result re-validated by pinning every
  placement into a freshly built model and asking CP-SAT.
* **WHAT IT DISPLACES** -- the same affected list the delta card reads. Under a
  held-world price this is usually EMPTY, and the answer says so plainly rather
  than falling silent, with an honest pointer at the deeper search and no promise
  about what it would find.
* **WHETHER THE DUE DATE SURVIVES** -- the recomputed service outcome, with R-PD1
  clause (4)'s split intact. **The floor is stated ONCE**, as unmovable: reporting
  it before AND after would invite a reader to subtract two equal numbers and
  read a decision into a fact no placement can change.

New on `LocalPrice`: `subject_outcomes`, populated only when a caller names the
demands it cares about, so nothing changes for a caller that does not ask.

## 5. ITEM 4 -- THE REFUSAL BRANCHES, WHICH ARE THE ROUTE

Every branch has authored copy that says WHICH one happened and what it means.
The governing distinction, and it is the same one 4B.18 and 4B.23 ruled at other
seams:

> **CHUNKED and UNPRICEABLE are facts about OUR PROCESS. COLLISION, PRECEDENCE,
> CLOSURE, FROZEN and NO-LATER-FIT are facts about the PLANT.** A planner acts on
> those differently, and a surface that flattens them tells someone their plant
> refuses something nobody has tested.

**(a) COLLISION.** Live:

    Pushing ORD-000042 op10 out by "a day" puts it at Wednesday 2026-01-07 17:52
    on CUT-02.

    Not there: CUT-02 is already carrying ORD-000158 at that time.
    That is a refusal about this price, not about your plant: I hold every other
    job exactly where it is, so a slot somebody else is in is closed to me. The
    full re-solve is free to move them, and might.

    The next opening that WOULD take it is Thursday 2026-01-08 17:57, and that
    one costs $0.
      Nothing else moves there either.

`holds_others` is the ruled species a SIXTH time (`CostProof.unreadable` 4B.18,
`partitions()` 4B.21, `FeasibilityGhost.verdict` 4B.23, the 4B.24 refusal, the
4B.24 fifth). SHUT is not OCCUPIED, and a refusal that exists only because the
price holds the occupant still is "not without moving other work", never "no".

The alternative is **COMPUTED, not the minute after the obstacle** -- the minute
past a collision is very often inside the next one -- and it is **PRICED**, so
the offer is a number rather than a gesture at one.

**(b) CLOSED CALENDAR.** Live:

    Testing ORD-000246 op10 at Wednesday 2026-01-14 07:00 on CUT-01 - you said
    "Wednesday".
    There are 6 of that weekday still ahead of where it sits, and I took the
    first one. Name a date if you meant a different one.

    Not there: CUT-01 is not open then - planned maintenance from 2026-01-14
    00:00 to 2026-01-14 23:59.
    That is the calendar, not congestion: nobody is in the way, the machine
    simply is not running. Declared overtime or a shift extension is what changes
    it, and I won't guess what hours the plant would open on a day it has
    declared shut.

**(c) CHUNKED -- A PROCESS LIMIT, SAID AS ONE.** Live:

    ORD-000177 op10 runs in 2 pieces on CUT-01 around calendar closures, and I
    can't re-place split work as one move yet - the pauses are placements the
    solver made, and shifting the bar wouldn't tell me where the new ones go.
    That is a limit of mine, not a ruling about your plant: I am not telling you
    the move is impossible, I am telling you I can't price it. Dragging the bar
    on the board runs the full re-solve, which can.

Checked from the ROW, before the model build: the pricer declines it anyway
(4B.24's stated limit), and paying ~6.5 s to be told what the row already says is
waste. A guard asserts the pricer is never reached.

**(d) FROZEN / PINNED.** The brief expected this to be unreachable for a later
move of active work. **It is not**: on the demo board every bar starting
2026-01-05 is inside the committed front, so ordinary questions about ordinary
orders land here. Live:

    ORD-000132 op10 starts 2026-01-05 14:18, inside the committed front of the
    plan (frozen until 2026-01-06 00:00) [docs/05 R-F1]. Committed work does not
    move as the plan rolls - that is what committing it means - so there is no
    later slot for me to price. Moving the frozen boundary is the gesture that
    changes this, and it makes standing pins, not free work.

A separate A7/F1 branch covers an explicit lock.

**(e) NOTHING LATER, and PAST THE GRID.** Two different facts. "No later stretch
of open, unheld time big enough" is measured from the machine's own free time;
"past the end of the planned grid" is measured against the MODEL HORIZON.

**A CORRECTION MADE INSIDE THIS SESSION:** the first version measured that
against the ROLLING WINDOW END, and it was wrong. The demo board's window closes
2026-01-15 while the schedule places work in detail out to February, so the
specimen's own target -- Thursday 2026-01-15 07:00, four days inside the placed
grid -- was refused as *"past what this schedule plans"*. The window bounds what
a planner is looking AT; the horizon bounds what the model has a variable for,
and only the second one bounds a price.

**PRECEDENCE GOT ITS OWN BRANCH, because the remedy inverts.** A later
alternative unblocks a busy slot and makes a boxed-in one WORSE, so this branch
offers none and says why. An offer that helps nothing is worse than no offer.

## 6. THE PRICE WAS BEING ASKED THE WRONG QUESTION (found by a zero-distance move)

Not in the brief, and the session would have shipped a wrong number without it.

The first working version priced against the snapshot unrestricted. As a sanity
check I priced a ZERO-DISTANCE move -- the bar left exactly where it already is
-- and got:

    priced: False
    refusal: {'family': 'model', 'sentence': "the scheduler refuses this
              placement with every other job held where it is, and I can't name
              which rule stops it"}
    validation: {'status': 'INFEASIBLE'}

The refusal was real and it was about the wrong question. A rolling board's
snapshot carries every operation the plant knows about -- **695 on the demo
board** -- and its schedule placed **386**. Pinning those 386 into an
unrestricted model asks CP-SAT whether the other 309 can also be placed inside
this horizon, which the rolling solve never asked and whose answer here is no.

The API's own drag path has always restricted a gesture to the window's op set
and held the frozen front (`_rolling_gesture_context`, 4B.3c CU3). The ask path
now reads the same two facts off the same document, so **the ASK path prices the
same world the DRAG path prices**. Not an optimization; the difference between a
price and a false refusal.

**No new intent.** `what-would-change` owns both directions, because both are the

## 10. NOT FIXED, NAMED

**(a) THE PRICEABLE CASE IS RARE ON THIS BOARD, AND THE SESSION MEASURED HOW
RARE.** Of the seven live later-turns, ONE priced directly and two more priced
their offered alternative; the rest refused. That is the correct answer on a
board at 92.4% gap with 386 bars, and 4B.27 predicted it. But it means the
priced branch's copy has had far less live exercise than the refusal copy, and
the R-PD1 floor/controllable wording was verified on ONE order (ORD-000208).

**(b) A TWO-OPERATION MOVE CANNOT BE PRICED, AND THAT IS THE SPECIMEN'S OWN
ANSWER.** Turn 1 refuses on precedence: what the planner actually needs is to
move op10 AND op20. The pricer holds one bar; there is no gesture, on the board
or on the ask path, that moves a routing. Naming it is honest and it is also the
most valuable thing this session found out.

**(c) `op_seq` IS NOT PARSEABLE.** "ORD-000208 op30" reaches the route as the
order alone; the operation comes only from a BOARD SELECTION. Every multi-op
order therefore answers about its FIRST operation unless a bar is selected -- the
route says so ("the first of its 4 operations"), which is honest and is not the
same as answering the question. This is a parse-contract change (an operation
subject kind) and was out of scope.

**(d) THE ALTERNATIVE OFFER CAN COST A SECOND MODEL BUILD.** `validate_held_world`
consumes the model, so a second price of the same world rebuilds it. Measured
walls on the demo board: 1.3-6.8 s per turn, most of it the first build, which
is amortised across the conversation by the Explainer's cached world. A cold
first later-question is the slow one.

**(e) THE MAGNITUDE VOCABULARY IS AUTHORED AND FINITE.** minutes/hours/days/
weeks/fortnight/months, digits and number words to ten. "a couple of shifts",
"till end of month", "after the holiday" all fall back -- and SAY they fell back,
which is the ruled behaviour, but it is a fallback.
