**THIS INVERTED THE EXPLANATION WE CAME IN WITH.** 4B.23 said beat one's 2-second
budget was marginal. It was -- but not because the search is expensive. The
verdict costs **four hundredths of a deterministic unit**, the same figure to
four decimals at every seed, while spending **two and a half to four seconds of
wall**. That wall is presolve, which the deterministic meter barely counts. So
raising the deterministic budget alone would have changed nothing at all; the
number that had to move was the WALL.

`FEASIBILITY_BUDGET_S` 2.0 -> **12.0** (a ceiling at 2.5x the measured worst
case). `FEASIBILITY_DET_TIME_S` 1.0 -> **2.0** (47x the measured cost), chosen so
the deterministic budget is what stops the search on any board.

### (d) The wall-per-unit exchange rate

**40.8 / 44.9 / 52.2 / 53.4 / 77.0 seconds per deterministic unit** at a 1.0
budget (median 52.2), and **33.9 s/unit** at a 3.0 budget -- the fixed presolve
cost amortizes, so the rate improves with the budget. Range **33.9 to 77.0**.

That rate is what makes the wall dangerous as a ceiling, and it is now derived
rather than guessed: `wall_ceiling_for()` gives a deterministic re-solve the
larger of the caller's ceiling and `det_budget x 120 s/unit`. 120 is the measured
worst case with half again on top, so only a machine running at half the slowest
rate ever observed can be stopped by the clock. **"Wall as SAFETY CEILING only"
is now enforced, not merely intended** -- and `applied_time_limit_s` reports the
limit actually applied, which is the question that field exists to answer.

**`sandbox.price_drop`** is what `POST /sandbox` now calls. `sandbox_pin_resolve`

**Clause (1)** reaches four solve sites: beat one (seeded, deterministic budget),
the baseline/opportunity, the accept re-solve, and the audit. A wall-truncated
solve **refuses to price** rather than pricing from a draw, in the same register

plant. Beat one's undetermined sentence now says WHICH budget ran out, because a
deterministic stop is reproducible (retrying gets the same non-answer) and a wall
stop is not (retrying may well answer).

**Clause (2) reached the accept path, and had to.** The card promises "nothing
else moved". Accept re-solved the window freely, so a planner could say yes to
one schedule and be handed another. `apply_planner_edit` takes
`hold_all_placements` (default True from the API) and pins every incumbent
placement. The promise on the card and the schedule that lands are now the same
object.

**`holds_others` is the load-bearing bit of a refusal, and it is the same species
this session exists to end.** A machine that is SHUT refuses the pin however the
plan is arranged. A machine that is merely OCCUPIED refuses it only because this
price holds the occupant still. Reporting the second as the first would state a
fact about our METHOD as a fact about the PLANT -- `CostProof`'s fourth state
(4B.18), `partitions()` tri-state (4B.21), `FeasibilityGhost.verdict` (4B.23),
now a fifth time. Item 6(b), verbatim:

```

        blocking=['ORD-000123'] at=2026-01-21T07:00:00+00:00 holds_others=True

                every other job exactly where it is, so the scheduler might still
                fit it here by moving other work"
```

**Clause (3)/(4)/(5):** `POST /audit` (the deliberate search) and

## 6. WHAT WAS TEMPTING AND LEFT

**Falling through to the re-solve when the local price refuses.** A collision
drop used to produce a card charging the displaced order (4B.22a's "displacement
that costs something", $2,301.67 of $2,596.67). Under clause (2) it is a refusal.
It was tempting to keep the old behaviour behind the refusal -- price it by
re-solving when the held world says no. That is the lottery walking back in
through a side door, and the brief rules on it explicitly (Item 6(b) EXPECTS a
refusal). What the card does instead is say the true thing: the spot is taken by
ORD-000123, and holding everything still is why we cannot price it -- the
scheduler may be able to make room, which is a different question.

**Picking seed 44.** On the demo board seed 44 finds 16.3% and seed 42 finds
nothing at one deterministic unit. Choosing the seed that wins on the board in
front of you is exactly the un-principled thing, and it would have made the audit
look better than it is. Seed 42 stands; the finding is reported below.

**Running the opportunity search on every drag.** Clause (3) renders it; at 33.9
to 77.0 seconds per unit, making a planner pay for it on a gesture would have
undone the 150x the local price just bought. It is opt-in per request and is what
"search deeper" runs.

**Caching the model build.** It is now 60-80% of the local price's wall and 100%
of what is left to optimise on this path. 4B.23 named and priced it and declined
it for the right reason: a stale cache prices a gesture against the wrong model.
Unchanged here.

## 7. REPORTED, NOT FIXED

**(a) BEAT ONE IS NOW THE WHOLE COST OF A GESTURE.** The founder's drag is ~8.2
seconds end to end, of which beat two is 0.38s. Beat one is 4.8-5.7s: a ~2.4s
model build plus a ~2.4s solve that consumes 0.0426 deterministic units. Almost
all of it is presolve. The obvious move -- price locally in beat one too, since
the local pricer already answers "does this fit" in 27ms -- is a real option this
session did not take, because beat one's job is deliberately a RELAXATION (it
answers whether the plant has room if other work moves, which is precisely the
question the local price cannot answer, and Item 6(b) depends on the difference).

**(b) THE AUDIT'S SEED FINDS NOTHING AT ONE UNIT AND $239,824.80 AT THREE.** Both
the seed and the budget decide, and not merely by running longer -- CP-SAT
schedules its search portfolio around the budget it is handed, so a bigger budget
is a DIFFERENT search. Multi-seed portfolios are out of scope by the brief and
are now a follow-on with a measured case behind them.

**(c) A CHUNKED OPERATION CANNOT BE PRICED LOCALLY.** Its pauses are calendar
closures the solver placed and a local shift cannot re-derive them. The pricer
declines by name ("this operation runs in chunks around calendar closures; moving
it needs the full re-solve, not a local price") rather than inventing a chunk
pattern. On the demo board 1 operation of 386 is chunked, so this is rare and
loud rather than common and quiet.

**(d) `POST /audit/accept` IS SMOKE-TESTED, NOT PROVEN.** The offer path is
verified live (Item 6(e)). `materialize_audit_offer` was run end to end on a
temporary fixture -- model build, standing pins, deterministic solve, extraction
into a child snapshot -- and reached its clean refusal ("the deeper search did not
beat the incumbent on this run - nothing accepted"), which is the correct branch
on an already-optimal board. **The SUCCESS branch -- minting a child schedule from
a real improvement -- has not been executed**, because running it against the demo
board mints a schedule row in the working data root. It is the one piece of this
session's surface whose happy path has no execution behind it.

**(e) ITEM 6 WAS DRIVEN THROUGH THE API, NOT A POINTER.** The Chrome extension was
not connected this session, so the five gestures were driven through the exact
request sequence `controller.js` issues (same endpoints, same feel-token budgets,
same correlation id), not through real drags in a browser. The frontend's own
reading rules are guarded by Playwright instead. A browser pass is owed.

**(f) `tests/test_rolling_two_beat.py` IS RED AT HEAD.** 12 errors,
`build_rolling_view() got an unexpected keyword argument 'det_time'`. Verified
pre-existing by stashing this session's changes and re-running. Not from this
session and not fixed here.

**(g) THE MOVED ORDER APPEARS IN ITS OWN AFFECTED LIST** when the moved operation
is the last step of its order (the fixture's shape, and the pinned world's
Item 6(d)). The founder's own bar had a downstream step that absorbed the shift,
so his correct list was empty. Both are right; the general property the guard
asserts is that **no order but the planner's own is touched**.

**(h) docs/04's ENTRY WAS CORRECTED BEFORE COMMIT.** The first draft of the
amendment claimed the search plateaus and that seed 42 finds nothing; the 3.0-unit
probe and the constant-`DeterministicTime()` discovery landed afterwards and
contradicted both. The entry was corrected in place, in the same session, before
it became history. The append-only rule protects committed history; an entry
being finished is not history yet.
