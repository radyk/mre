## 2. Q1 -- DOES THE COLD PORTFOLIO BECOME WORTH SOMETHING AT A LARGER BUDGET?

**YES. Emphatically, and the value switches on between 6.0 and 10.0 units.**

Cold main-solve portfolio value against the incumbent, K=5:

```
   3.0 units ->        $578.16   (0.027%)
   6.0 units ->      $1,298.60   (0.061%)
  10.0 units ->    $460,014.78   (21.6%)      <- 354x the 6.0 figure
  15.0 units ->    $460,014.78   (21.6%)      <- the winner PLATEAUS
```

**4B.25 section 7(a)'s open question resolves toward BUDGET STARVATION, not
cold-start geometry.** The cold members were not landing in the same poor
region -- they had not been given enough search to leave it. For scale: the
warm audit at 3.0 units found $545,549.60 (4B.25 section 2). Cold reaches
**84% of the warm figure** given roughly 3.3x the budget. The warm start is
worth a large multiple of budget, which is exactly what a warm start is for --
but it is not the difference between a lever that works and one that does not.

**THE WINNER PLATEAUS AT 10 UNITS; THE MEMBERS DO NOT.** 15.0 finds the same
winner, seed 44, at the same ledger **to the cent** ($1,667,467.80). But three
of five seeds (43, 44, 46) return ledgers IDENTICAL to their 10.0 values while
seed 42 improves 16.2% ($2,135,369.63 -> $1,790,404.72) and seed 45 improves
0.25%. So per-seed convergence is uneven and mostly complete by 10 units, and
the extra 5 units bought the portfolio nothing at all.

**THE SPREAD DOES NOT COLLAPSE -- IT WIDENS AND THEN NARROWS.** 1.77% at 3.0,
1.81% at 6.0, **28.06%** at 10.0, 25.20% at 15.0. The tight spread at low budget
was never agreement; it was three starved searches all failing in the same
place. R-BK1 clause (4) publishes this figure, and on this board it is honest in
both directions.

**AND COLD PORTFOLIOS ARE NOT STRUCTURALLY WEAK ANYWHERE THE BOARD SOLVES.**
The mid board at w10, at the SHIPPED 6.0 units, spreads **42.10%**
($427,464.47) across five cold seeds. 4B.25's "$578" is a fact about the demo
board at a starved budget, not a fact about cold search.

## 3. Q2 -- HOW RELIABLE IS THE EMPTY-BOARD HAZARD, AND DOES K INSURE?

**Empty cells: 9 of 30.** On the demo board, **4 of 20** -- seeds 44 and 45, at
3.0 and at 6.0, and nowhere else. On the mid board, **5 of 10** -- every seed at
w14, none at w10.

**Is seed 44 empty at 6.0? YES.** Same two seeds as at 3.0; doubling the budget
rescued neither.
**Is seed 44 empty at 10.0? NO -- and it WINS.** The seed that publishes nothing
at the shipped budget produces the cheapest board in the whole sweep. The hazard
is not a bad seed. It is a seed that had not been given enough search.

**At the SHIPPED default (K=1, 6.0 units, seed 42): DOES THIS BOARD PUBLISH?**

> **YES at seed 42** -- $2,127,482.58, which is the incumbent's ledger TO THE
> CENT, so the shipped configuration is confirmed to be exactly what is
> registered.
> **YES at seed 43** ($2,164,606.83).
> **NO at seed 44. NO at seed 45.** Both return UNKNOWN with zero bars.
> **YES at seed 46** ($2,126,183.98).
>
> **Two of five seeds would publish an empty board at the shipped default.**

**WHICH SEED FAILS IS A PROPERTY OF (BOARD x BUDGET), NOT OF THE SEED.** 44 and
45 fail on the demo board at <=6.0 and nobody fails at >=10.0; on the mid board
at w14 everybody fails and at w10 nobody does. There is no "good seed" to pick
in advance, and that -- not any particular seed's record -- is the argument that
a fixed seed0 is a gamble.

**BUT K IS NOT UNIVERSAL INSURANCE, AND THE MID BOARD PROVES IT.** At
mid170-w14 every one of five seeds returns UNKNOWN. No K insures against a
window that is uniformly out of reach; K=5 there buys 806.5 seconds and an empty
board. Where the window IS reachable the hazard is seed-wise and K does insure.
Those are two different regimes and a K default has to be argued for both.

**THE WINDOW, NOT THE DENSITY, KILLS THE 170-ORDER BOARD.** Same world, same
budget, same seeds: **w14 gives 0 of 5, w10 gives 5 of 5.** Running only the
w14 configuration the errand named would have supported "the hazard is worse one
density down", which is false -- one density down at the demo board's own window
is the healthiest arm measured (5 of 5, and the largest spread in the sweep).
This is 4B.22a's non-monotone solvability and docs/07 section 5a.15's
budget-starved 14-day window, seen from the seed axis.

**the budget lever is unsafe alone and safe with K.**

## 5. THE RECOMMENDATION

> **K=3 at the main solve, and the per-member budget raised from 6.0 to 10.0
> deterministic units -- as ONE change, because neither half is safe alone.**

Argued from the table:

**(1) K=3 buys everything K=5 buys, where it matters.** Same winner, same ledger
to the cent, at 10.0, at 15.0, and on the mid board. K=5's advantage appears
only at starved budgets and is worth $578-$1,299 (<=0.06%) -- below anything a
planner would act on. 4B.25 reached the same conclusion for `AUDIT_K` from
different evidence, so the two coefficients agree rather than being tuned apart.

**(2) K=3 costs 45% of K=5's wall** -- 584.6s against 1308.0s of member time at
10.0 units, sequential. (Not the 60% the member count suggests, because member
walls are uneven: K=5 carries seed 46's 519.8s outlier.) A persisting solve adds
one winner re-solve, giving **775.6s = 12.9 minutes sequential, or ~9.3 minutes
on three processes** at 4B.25's measured 1.8x contention penalty -- **3.9x the
shipped K=1 at 6.0** (198.6s, no re-solve at K=1).

**(3) K is what makes the budget raise safe.** At K=1 the raise costs $7,887.05;
at K=3 it earns $460,014.78. Recommending 10.0 units without K would be
recommending a regression on the only board we have measured it on.

**(4) K>=2 is required for R-BK1 clause (4) to say anything at all.** A
single-member portfolio has no spread -- correctly, `spread_abs` is None below
two publishable members. At K=1 the certificate cannot tell a planner that this
window is far from settled, and on this board at 10 units it is 28% from
settled. That sentence is free at K=3 and unobtainable at K=1.

**(5) Three chances, not one, at a publishable board.** Two of five seeds return
nothing at the shipped budget, and which two is a property of the board and the
budget rather than of the seed. K=3 does not eliminate the hazard -- mid170-w14
shows a regime where no K helps -- but it converts a single point of failure into
three independent draws wherever the window is reachable at all.

**What argues against it, stated plainly.** This is ONE board at one density
plus one control board, on one laptop. The $460,014.78 is measured against an
incumbent at a 92.4% gap, so it is a large improvement on a board nobody has
proved anything about -- the ledger is real money and the gap is unchanged by
any of this. K=3 x 10.0 units is 3.9x the shipped solve's wall, which is
a real cost to a planner pressing "search deeper", and 4B.25 section 7(c)
already noted nothing in the cockpit says a solve is now several searches long.
**Daryn decides; this errand argues.**

## 6. REPORTED, NOT FIXED

**(a) MORE BUDGET IS NOT MONOTONE IN THE LEDGER FOR A FIXED SEED.** Seed 43:
$2,164,599.48 at 3.0 -> $2,164,606.83 at 6.0 (+$7.35). Seed 42: $2,127,482.58 at
6.0 -> $2,135,369.63 at 10.0 (+$7,887.05). A deeper search returning a dearer
schedule is not a defect in the solver -- CP-SAT schedules its portfolio around
the budget it is handed (4B.24 section 5a.98) -- but it means "raise the budget"
is not a safe unilateral improvement, and nothing in the product says so.

**(b) THE 15-UNIT ARM BOUGHT NOTHING AND COST 638 SECONDS.** Same winner, same
ledger to the cent as 10.0, with three of five members returning byte-identical
ledgers. Where the per-seed plateau actually sits between 6.0 and 10.0 was not
bisected; 8.0 was not measured.

**(c) THE COLD-VS-WARM GAP IS NARROWED, NOT CLOSED.** Cold at 10.0 units
recovers 84% of the warm audit's 3.0-unit figure. Whether the residual 16% is
the warm start's remaining advantage or the different objective the audit
optimises was not measured. 4B.25 section 7(a) is downgraded from "unexplained"
to "mostly budget", not discharged.

**(d) mid170-w14's 0-of-5 WAS NOT PUSHED TO A LARGER BUDGET.** The demo board's
hazard cleared between 6.0 and 10.0; whether the mid board's w14 window clears at
10.0 too, or is genuinely out of reach, is one arm nobody ran. If it clears, the
"two regimes" framing in section 3 is really one regime with a budget threshold
that moves with the window.

**(e) EVERY FIGURE HERE IS ONE LAPTOP, AND THE SWEEP WAS INTERRUPTED ONCE.** The
first process was killed by a tool timeout after completing `demo-6.0`; the
remainder ran in a second detached process with `--resume`. Ledgers are
unaffected (deterministic mode, and seed 42 at 6.0 reproduces the API-minted
incumbent to the cent across sessions and processes), but the WALL column spans
two processes. An idle `uvicorn` dev API was resident throughout (25s CPU over
four hours -- negligible, but named rather than assumed).

**(f) THE 519.8s AND 740.3s MEMBER WALLS ARE UNEXPLAINED.** demo-10.0 seed 46
took 519.8s where its four siblings took ~190-205s, and demo-15.0 seed 42 took
740.3s against ~250-380s. Both consumed their declared deterministic budget
exactly, so the deterministic result is unaffected -- this is the wall/det
exchange-rate variance 4B.24 measured at 33.9-77.0 s/unit, at the wide end. Not
investigated.

**(g) THE SWEEP MEASURES THE LEDGER AND SAYS NOTHING ABOUT THE GAP.** Every
publishable cell is FEASIBLE; no cell proved anything. A portfolio selects by
the ledger (R-BK1 clause 3) and this errand did likewise, so "21.6% cheaper"
means cheaper, not closer to optimal.
