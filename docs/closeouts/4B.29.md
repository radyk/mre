## 1a. THE mid170-w14 QUESTION -- ANSWERED, AND THE ANSWER IS "OUT OF REACH"

4B.26 §6(d) left this open: the demo board's hazard cleared between 6 and 10
units, so did the 170-order board's 14-day window clear at 10 too -- making
"two regimes" really one regime with a moving threshold?

**NO. FIFTEEN OF FIFTEEN CELLS ARE EMPTY** -- every seed, at 3, 6 AND 10
deterministic units. **The two regimes are real.** Where a window is reachable
the hazard is seed-wise and K insures against it; where a window is out of
reach, no K and no budget helps, and the honest output is a profile that says
*"this window is not reliably reachable at any measured budget"* and recommends
the deepest one that is. That sentence is not a fallback -- it is the finding,
and the 170-order board is the specimen that made the rule earn it.

**AND THE SAME WORLD AT TEN DAYS IS THE HEALTHIEST ARM MEASURED IN EITHER
SESSION:** 5 of 5 at every budget. 4B.26's *"the window, not the density, kills
it"* is confirmed at a budget 67% larger than the one that first showed it.

## 3. THE FLIP -- K=3 AT THE MAIN SOLVE, AND THE BUDGET LEFT ALONE

`SolveRequest.portfolio_k` defaults to **3**. `DET_TOTAL_DEFAULT` stays **6.0**.

This is **publication insurance, not optimization**, and the numbers say so: at
6.0 units K=3 buys about $578 on the demo board, 0.027% of ledger. What it buys
is that the board publishes at all -- 4B.26 measured **two of five seeds
returning an EMPTY BOARD** at the shipped default, and measured that WHICH two
is a property of (board x budget) rather than of the seed. There is no good seed
to pick in advance, so K=1 is a single point of failure with a measured ~40%
miss rate at this density.

**The budget did not move with it, deliberately.** 10.0 units is the DEMO
BOARD's knee. Its control board in the same sweep does not want a bigger budget
at all -- it wants a different window. Shipping 10.0 universally would be fitting
one board: the seed-44 mistake one level up.

**What the flip touched (Item 1(e), enumerated, not assumed).**

|---------|--------|-----|
| `tests/fixtures/baselines/rolling_pilot_golden.json` | NO | built by `build_rolling_view`, a module-level call whose K default is still 1 |
| every monolithic golden / `sample_data` baseline | NO | the portfolio lives only on the sliced path |
| `rolling-c362baa4-1b0` (pinned exam world) | NO | registered artifact; digest asserted unchanged |
| `rolling-c9973708-865` (demo board) | NO | registered artifact; digest asserted unchanged |

A code added without a planner-facing phrase, a remediation stance and a count is not added |

| `tools/build_rolling_exam_run.py`, `mint_demo_board.py` | pinned to K=1 | they exist to REPRODUCE two K=1 artifacts |

**THE LIBRARY DEFAULT IS STILL 1 AND THAT IS THE WHOLE REASON NO GOLDEN MOVED.**
`portfolio.DEFAULT_K` and `solve_rolling_portfolio(k=...)` stay at 1; only
`SolveRequest.portfolio_k` -- the number a request gets when it says nothing --
became 3. Two constants, two audiences.

**A DEFAULT CANNOT BREAK A REQUEST THAT WORKED BEFORE IT -- CAUGHT IN REVIEW,
NOT BY A TEST.** `SolveRequest.deterministic` defaults to **False**, and
`solve_rolling_portfolio` correctly REFUSES a portfolio of non-deterministic
members (R-BK1 clause 1: the best of K draws is a number with no meaning). So
the flip, as first written, turned every naive `{"sliced": true}` request into a
FAILED RUN. No existing test would have caught it: all three sliced API fixtures
pass `deterministic: true`, because everything this project measures is
deterministic by house rule. The DEFAULTED K now degrades to 1 with the reason
on the certificate; an EXPLICITLY requested portfolio still raises, because a
caller who asked for the best of three draws asked for something incoherent.
Guarded end to end.

**ON A PROVABLE BOARD THE FLIP CHANGES NOTHING BUT THE WALL.** Measured, 20
pilot_scale orders through the API at the new default: three members, all
OPTIMAL, all at $885.58, winner seed 42 by the lowest-seed tie-break. So the
board K=3 publishes is the board K=1 published, and the certificate gains
*"all 3 seeded searches landed on the same total"* -- R-BK1 clause (4)'s trust
statement, free. That is why the three sliced API fixtures pinned to K=1 above
would have passed unpinned; they were pinned for wall, not for outcome. It is a
property of boards that PROVE, and the demo board does not prove.

**A provenance bug the flip would have introduced, caught.** `k_declared` was
computed as `req.portfolio_k != DEFAULT_K`. After the flip that stamps every
DEFAULT solve as "declared", i.e. as a customer's choice. It now reads
`req.model_fields_set` -- what the request actually said -- which is the only
thing that answers "who chose this?".

## 4. R-CAL1 -- THE FOUR RULES, AND WHERE EACH ONE BITES

Verbatim in docs/04. What each one cost to make true:

**(1) MEASURED, NEVER AUTHORED.** The profile carries a sha256 of its own grid,
recomputed on load. An edited grid is `unreadable`, never `absent` -- a claim
about our calibration is never manufactured from a fact about our storage
(4B.18, again). The digest deliberately covers the MEASUREMENT and not the
bookkeeping, which is what makes re-use legal: 30 of this session's 55 cells are
4B.26's own rows, imported with `source` naming where they came from. Rule (1)
forbids anonymity, not re-use.

**(2) OFFERED, NEVER AUTO-APPLIED.** `--accept` takes a name and refuses a blank
one: a signature, not a flag. Accepting a grid whose digest does not match is
refused too -- that is the one place a human's authority could otherwise launder
a hand-edited number. **THE CALLER ALWAYS WINS**: a request declaring its own K
gets it, and the certificate then says the profile did not override it rather
than implying it applied.

**THE WINDOW IS NEVER OFFERED, AND THAT IS A RULING.** A window decides which
work is on the board -- it is what a planner asked to SEE, not a coefficient of
how hard we look. Silently re-cutting a 14-day horizon to a calibrated 10-day
one would answer a question nobody asked. What the certificate DOES carry is
`window_calibrated` beside `window_solved`, because mid170 is the specimen:
same world, same budget, 5 of 5 at ten days and 0 of 5 at fourteen.

**(3) DECLARED -- INCLUDING WHEN THE ANSWER IS NO.** `solver.calibration` is
PRESENT on an uncalibrated plant and says so. This is the opposite discipline
from `solver.portfolio`, which is absent by construction at K=1, and the
difference is deliberate: "nobody has measured this plant" is a fact a planner
should have, where "this board came from one search" is what every board has
always been. The block IS absent where the assembler was given no lookup at all
-- every module-level assembly -- which is why the bump moved no golden.

**(4) THE FACILITY IS THE SCOPE.** A submission declaring two facilities is
REFUSED calibration by name, pointing at the partition ruling. Not deferred, not
approximated -- refused, because one grid over two plants is calibrated for
neither.

**THE COEFFICIENTS ARE PRODUCT-SIDE, NOT IDS, AND THE ERRAND ASKED FOR THAT
ANSWER EXPLICITLY.** K, the per-member budget and the window are facts about OUR
SEARCH -- how long our solver looks and how many times. A plant cannot declare
them because a plant does not have them. Contrast rho (docs/06 §5.9): a capacity
derate is a fact about the PLANT, so it is a declared IDS coefficient and pays
the §8 pipeline-proof chain. Nothing in a profile reaches the model, the
objective or the ledger. **No docs/06 doorway is owed**, and docs/04 carries that
sentence so it is a decision on the record rather than an omission.

## 5. THE KNEE RULE

> the smallest measured budget at which **(i)** every seeded search published a
> board and **(ii)** its winning ledger is within a declared tolerance
> (default 1%) of the best winner at any LARGER measured budget

Condition (ii) is vacuously true at the largest measured budget. That is the
rule's own limit stated rather than hidden: a knee is the smallest budget as
good as anything MEASURED above it, never a claim about budgets nobody ran, and
the profile prints which budgets it measured.

Where no budget satisfies (i), the profile says **"this window is not reliably
reachable at any measured budget"** and recommends the deepest window that is,
naming the one it gave up on.

The recommended K comes off the same grid: members are consecutive seeds, so the
K-member portfolio of a measured arm IS the first K of its seeds -- no re-solve
is needed to read K=1, 2, 3 off a K=5 measurement. **K is floored at 2**, and the
profile says WHY in a separate field: where one seed captures all the value the
second member is not buying a cheaper board, it is buying R-BK1 clause (4)'s
spread sentence. An argument must not wear a measurement's clothes.

## 6. DRIFT

Under an accepted AND applied profile, a solve whose portfolio put fewer than K
searches on the board raises `CALIBRATION_DRIFT` (INFO / `proceeded_flagged`),
carries it on the certificate, and **publishes the best available member
anyway**. A schedule the planner can use is worth more than a clean certificate;
the no-derate precedent already settled that absence is loud but not a gate
verdict change.

It fires ONLY under an accepted profile. A plant on product defaults has no
promise to drift from, and reporting drift there would turn an uncalibrated
plant into a broken one.

It is a new code because the two adjacent codes make different claims:
`SOLVER_NONOPTIMAL` is about the PROOF of this board, `DENSITY_LIMIT` is about
the PLANT, and this is about OUR OWN COEFFICIENTS going stale. Third instance of
the add-never-repurpose discipline in this vocabulary.

**THE CLAUSE-(4) FLOOR CAUGHT ITS OWN AUTHOR.** The end-to-end drift fixture was
written expecting a K=3 recommendation and got K=2: its three synthetic cells had
seed 42 cheapest, so the smallest prefix capturing the value is 1, and the floor
raised it to 2. The test was wrong and the rule was right -- which is the first
evidence that the floor is a live branch rather than a paragraph. The fixture now
puts the cheapest board on seed 44 so it exercises a genuine K=3.

**The premise test is a real solve.** Everything else about drift is arithmetic
over synthetic cells, and arithmetic cannot prove that a seeded deterministic
search ever comes back empty. A 120-order window at 0.12 deterministic units
does -- three of three members return UNKNOWN, well below the cliff (0.2 units
publishes 3 of 3 on the same world), so it is not a knife-edge fixture.

## 7. THE COUNTDOWN COPY

"Searching deeper" now states its SCALE -- how many searches, roughly how many
minutes -- composed server-side on `/meta` and rendered as text, the same rule
the cost-proof chip runs under. It also runs a real elapsed clock against that
expectation.

**IT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT SAY "running search 2 of 3".** The audit is one
request with no progress channel; a member counter there would be an animation
pretending to be telemetry. 4A.5c already named what a real ticking count needs
(streaming or background execution) and this session did not build it. What is
on screen is true: a declared member count, a forecast marked "about", and a
clock counting this browser's own wall.

## 8. NOT FIXED, NAMED

**(a) TWO SYNTHETIC WORLDS SHARE ONE PLANT KEY.** `demo_board` and the
170-order control both declare `facility_scope: ["F001"]` and
`source_system: "SyntheticERP vGen"`, so rule (4) keys them identically and one
profile would overwrite the other in a shared data root. For REAL plants this is
correct behaviour -- one facility, one calibration, re-measured over time -- but
for our own measurement worlds it is a hazard, and this session worked around it
by keeping the two profiles in separate directories rather than by fixing the
generator. A generator that emitted a distinguishing facility per scenario would
close it.

**(b) THE AUDIT'S K IS NOT CALIBRATED.** `AUDIT_K` is a constant 3 at 3.0 units.
A profile calibrates the MAIN solve and says nothing about "search deeper",
which is the other place K is spent and the one a planner triggers on purpose.
Whether the audit should read the profile is a design question this session did
not open.

**(c) MONOLITHIC SOLVES CARRY NO CALIBRATION BLOCK AT ALL.** The block is
emitted on the rolling path only, because a profile is measured over windows and
a monolithic run has none. Honest, and it means the rule-(3) guarantee -- an
absence is stated -- holds for sliced solves and is simply not in force for
monolithic ones. Nothing says so on a monolithic certificate.

**(d) THE PROFILE HAS NO EXPIRY.** R-CAL1 rule (3) speaks of an "expired"
profile and nothing computes one. Drift is the only staleness signal, and drift
only fires when a search actually fails -- a profile whose knee has quietly moved
UP while still publishing K boards is stale and silent. An age threshold is a
declared coefficient nobody has, which is R-PD1 clause (5)'s shape again.

**(e) EVERY WALL IN BOTH PROFILES IS ONE LAPTOP, AND THE MID170 ARM RAN BESIDE
THE TEST SUITE.** Ledgers are unaffected -- deterministic mode, and the imported
rows reproduce 4B.26's figures to the cent -- but the wall column of the mid170
arm was measured while the non-slow pytest suite ran in another process. 4B.25
measured a member running ~1.8x slower with siblings; this contention is smaller
than that and is named rather than assumed away.

**(f) THE CEREMONY IS A CLI AND NOTHING ELSE.** No Gatehouse surface, no
scheduling, no cloud execution, no re-calibration trigger. Deliberately out of
scope, and it means calibration is reachable by whoever has a shell and by
nobody else.

**(g) `--save` WRITES INTO A DATA ROOT AND NEITHER PROFILE WAS SAVED INTO THE
WORKING ONE.** Both are committed as artifacts under `docs/calibration/`, which
is where a reader can check them. Placing either where live solves would find it
is an act with consequences and belongs to the person who accepts it.

**(h) THE COST FORECAST OVERSHOT BY ~2.1x ON BOTH PLANTS.** Demo: projected
2200s for the five cells it had to measure, actually 991s. mid170: projected
7150s for twenty cells, actually 3464s. Both are ~25 s/deterministic unit,
BELOW 4B.24's measured 33.9-77.0 range -- so the exchange rate is a property of
the board as well as of the machine, and the ceremony's 55 s/unit constant is
now known to be conservative on these two. A planner told "about 37 minutes" who
waits 17 is not harmed, but a forecast that is wrong by 2x in a KNOWN direction
should be a measured per-plant figure rather than a constant. Not fixed: it
would need the first cell's rate to re-forecast the rest, which is a nicer
ceremony and a different one.

**(i) EVERY PUBLISHABLE CELL IN BOTH GRIDS IS FEASIBLE.** No cell proved
anything. The knee selects by the LEDGER (R-BK1 clause 3) and so does the
profile, so "10 units is better than 6" means cheaper, never closer to optimal.
4B.26 §6(g)'s caveat travels with this one unchanged.
