## 1. ITEM 3 -- THE MEMBER TABLE

Dense demo board's own submission (`demo_board`, 280 orders, seed 1, ref
2026-01-05, window 10 / frozen 1 -- the world `rolling-c9973708-865` was minted
from). K=5, per-member budget **3.0 deterministic units**, seeds 42-46,
PYTHONHASHSEED=0, workers=1 per member. Raw rows:
`tools/spikes/portfolio_4b25/measurements.jsonl`.

**Incumbent (the registered board): $2,127,482.58, FEASIBLE, gap 92.41%,
objective 163,448,443.**

| seed | ledger total | status | det consumed | wall (sequential) | wall (5 processes) |
|------|--------------|--------|--------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| 42 | $2,127,482.58 | FEASIBLE | 3.0700 | 91.7s | 166.4s |
| 43 | $2,164,599.48 | FEASIBLE | 3.1572 | 89.9s | 163.6s |
| 44 | *none* | **UNKNOWN** | 2.7500 | 62.6s | 120.8s |
| 45 | *none* | **UNKNOWN** | 2.7500 | 73.1s | 136.7s |
| 46 | **$2,126,904.42** | FEASIBLE | 3.0000 | 99.3s | 178.1s |

**WINNER: seed 46 at $2,126,904.42 -- $578.16 cheaper than the incumbent
(0.027%).**
**SPREAD: $37,695.06 = 1.77% of the winner**, across the three publishable
members.
**UNPUBLISHED: 2 of 5.**

**Wall totals.** Sequential **416.5s**; five processes **298.8s**. Those two
numbers are not directly comparable as measured and the accounting matters:

* Sequential = the sum of five member walls (416.5s) and NO winner re-solve,
  because the sequential path still holds the winning member's view in memory.
* Parallel = the slowest member (178.1s) + pool overhead + **the winner
  re-solved in the parent (~99s)**, because a parallel member's view lives and
  dies in its own process.
* **For a real (persisting) solve, both pay the re-solve:** sequential
  ~**515.8s**, parallel **298.8s**. **Speedup 1.73x on five processes.**

Not 5x, and the reason is on the table: **each member is ~1.8x SLOWER under
contention** (seed 46: 99.3s alone, 178.1s with four siblings). Five
single-worker CP-SAT solves on one laptop compete for memory bandwidth, not for
cores. That is the number the cloud-box decision should be made against, and it
is a laptop's number -- a box with real per-core memory bandwidth will do better,
and how much better is not something this machine can measure.

### The three findings in that table

**(a) TWO OF FIVE SEEDS RETURN AN EMPTY BOARD.** Seeds 44 and 45 spend their
whole stage-1 cap (2.75 units = 3.0 minus the R-SC3 reserve) and return UNKNOWN
with no ledger at all. This is 4B.22a's non-monotone solvability, seen from a new
angle: at this density, whether the window solves is a property of the SEED. A
portfolio that quietly shrank from five members to three would have reported a
1.77% spread and said nothing about the 40% of its own search that produced
nothing -- which is exactly what clause (4) exists to prevent.

**(b) SEED 42 REPRODUCES THE INCUMBENT'S LEDGER TO THE CENT** --
$2,127,482.58, the registered board's own figure, from a fresh spine run in a
scratch directory. An independent confirmation that the demo board is a seed-42
deterministic solve and that this harness is measuring the same object.

**(c) THE MAIN-SOLVE PORTFOLIO BUYS $578, NOT 16%.** 4B.24's 13-16% was measured
on a WARM-STARTED window re-solve (`baseline_window_solve` seeds the incumbent's
placements as hints). The main solve is COLD. Same board, same budgets, same
seeds -- and the lever is worth two orders of magnitude less without the warm
start. **This is the single most important result of the session for the K
default**, and it is an argument for K=1 at the main solve and K>1 at the audit,
which is where the brief said to build it first.

**Determinism, proven across execution modes.** The ledger totals AND the
deterministic times are **identical to ten decimal places between the sequential
and the process-parallel runs** (seed 42: `3.0700218750324186` in both). The wall
times differ by up to 80 seconds. Clause (1) and clause (5) proven together in
one table: parallelism BETWEEN runs changes nothing about any run. And across two
independent sequential runs of the whole portfolio (one before this session's
`det_consumed` instrumentation landed, one after), the five ledger totals were
identical while the wall totals differed by 55 seconds.

## 2. ITEM 2 -- THE AUDIT PORTFOLIO, LIVE

Same board, through the real `POST /schedules/{id}/audit`, against a SCRATCH
COPY of the data root. K=5, 3.0 deterministic units per member, seeds 42-46. Raw
rows: `tools/spikes/portfolio_4b25/audit_accept.jsonl`.

| seed | ledger total | vs incumbent | det consumed | wall (rep 0) | wall (rep 1) |
|------|--------------|--------------|--------------|--------------|--------------|
| 42 | $1,887,657.78 | −$239,824.80 | 3.000176 | 153.4s | 116.3s |
| 43 | $2,030,588.40 | −$96,894.18 | 3.005971 | 140.6s | 130.6s |
| 44 | **$1,581,932.98** | **−$545,549.60** | 3.000093 | 141.6s | 139.4s |
| 45 | $1,784,070.77 | −$343,411.81 | 3.000010 | 135.2s | 168.4s |
| 46 | $1,859,103.07 | −$268,379.51 | 3.000027 | 135.3s | 147.7s |

**SPREAD $448,655.42 = 28.36%. Wall 711.3s / 705.4s for the whole K=5 audit.**

**THE PORTFOLIO FOUND 2.3x MORE MONEY THAN THE SINGLE SEED.** 4B.24's audit
offered **$239,824.80** off this board. That was seed 42, and seed 42 is the
FOURTH-BEST of five. Seed 44 finds **$545,549.60**.

**AND SEED 42 REPRODUCES 4B.24's FIGURE EXACTLY** -- $2,127,482.58 −
$1,887,657.78 = $239,824.80, to the cent, from a different session on a
different day through a different entry point. Cross-session determinism, not
claimed but observed.

**K=3 FINDS THE SAME WINNER**, because seed 44 is in the first three -- which is
why the shipped `AUDIT_K` default is **3** and not 5: on this board it buys the
whole $545,549.60 for 60% of the wall.

**The offer sentence, verbatim:**

```
the search found a schedule $545,549.60 cheaper (best of 5 seeded searches at
3 deterministic units each (seeds 42-46); the 5 seeded searches spread 28.36%
apart - this window is far from settled) - review?
```

That parenthetical is clause (4). A planner reading "$545,549.60 cheaper" alone
would reasonably take it for the answer; reading that five independent searches
of the same window landed 28% apart, they know it is a floor and that the board
is nowhere near settled.

### ITEM 5(b) -- the same audit twice, identical

**2 repeats -> 1 DISTINCT OFFER**, compared on the full tuple (sentence, winning
seed, delta, moved-op count, the serialized affected list, every member's ledger
total, and the spread) -- the 4B.24 convention, not the headline alone. Member
deterministic times identical to six decimal places; member WALL times differ by
up to 37 seconds. Same statement as the main solve's, from the other endpoint.

## 3. WHAT SHIPPED

**`src/mre/modules/portfolio.py`** -- the primitive. The member record, the pure
selection (`min` over a fixed set under a total order: ledger, then seed), the
three authored agreement registers, the certificate block, and the runner whose
`workers > 1` path uses separate PROCESSES. It knows nothing about rolling views
or baselines; its callers hand it a member function.

**`rolling_horizon.solve_rolling_portfolio`** -- the main solve. K=1 is
`build_rolling_view` called once, returning no portfolio, with no extra solve and
no scratch directory. K>1 runs K probes, selects, and re-solves the winner with
the caller's `persist` -- and checks that re-solve's ledger against the member
that won, raising `PortfolioDrift` on a mismatch. That is clause (1) asserted
rather than assumed.

**`sandbox.audit_incumbent`** -- "search deeper", now K=3 by default. Every
member is published; the offer's sentence carries the declaration and the spread;
the offer carries the WINNING SEED so the accept can re-solve the right search.

**`sandbox.materialize_audit_offer`** gains `expect_delta_abs` and refuses to
mint a child whose delta does not match the offer to the cent.

**Contract 1.13** -- `solver.portfolio`, Optional, absent at K=1.

## 6. WHAT WAS TEMPTING AND LEFT

**Defaulting the main solve to K>1.** Item 3's table says it would buy $578.16 on
this board for 100 extra seconds of wall, and would mean every golden, digest and
pinned world lives behind a coefficient nobody has reviewed. Clause (2) exists to
make that Daryn's call with the table in front of him, and the table is above.

**Picking a seed.** Seed 44 wins the audit and seed 46 wins the main solve on
this board. Choosing either as the shipped `seed0` is 4B.24's refused temptation
wearing a different hat: it would tune the product to one board. Seeds stay
consecutive from 42.

**Making the losing members' PLACEMENTS available.** They are real schedules and
a planner might want to look at one. Every member would then have to be
extracted, persisted and registered -- five schedules per solve, five rows in the
registry, and a "which board am I on" question the cockpit has no answer for.
Clause (4) publishes their LEDGERS, which is what a spread needs, and nothing
more.

**Reusing one CpModel across members.** `_two_stage_solve` mutates the model
(stage 2 adds a bound and re-minimizes), so members must each build their own.
That is ~6.5s of the ~90s member wall on this board (4B.23 §5a.92) and the
obvious optimisation is the model cache 4B.23 and 4B.24 both named and both
declined for the same reason: a stale cache prices against the wrong model.

## 7. REPORTED, NOT FIXED

**(a) THE MAIN-SOLVE PORTFOLIO IS WORTH $578 AND THE AUDIT'S IS WORTH $545,549.**
Same board, same seeds, same budgets. The difference is the WARM START: the audit
re-solves from the incumbent's placements as hints, the main solve is cold. This
is the session's most useful result and it is not fully explained -- whether a
cold portfolio is weak because the members all land in the same poor region, or
because 3.0 units is barely enough to place 386 bars at all (two of five seeds
place NOTHING), is not measured here. A cold portfolio at a larger per-member
budget is the obvious next measurement and was not run.

**(b) TWO OF FIVE MAIN-SOLVE SEEDS RETURN AN EMPTY BOARD.** Seeds 44 and 45 spend
their whole stage-1 cap and return UNKNOWN. At K=1 with seed 44 the shipped
default would publish nothing at all -- 4B.22a's non-monotone solvability, now
known to be seed-dependent as well as size-dependent. Not investigated.

**(c) "SEARCH DEEPER" NOW COSTS ~7 MINUTES AT K=3** (~140s per member,
sequential) against ~2.4 minutes before. It is a declared coefficient and a
deliberate act, and `workers` shortens it on a box with cores -- but nothing on
the cockpit's searching state tells a planner it is now three searches long. The
countdown copy was not revisited.

**(d) THE PROCESS-PARALLEL SPEEDUP IS 1.73x ON FIVE PROCESSES, ON A LAPTOP.**
Each member runs ~1.8x slower with four siblings. This machine cannot tell you
whether that is memory bandwidth, thermal, or scheduler, and the cloud number is
therefore unknown rather than extrapolated.

**(e) A PARALLEL MEMBER RE-RUNS THE WHOLE SPINE.** `prepare_plant` costs ~1.4s
here and runs once per member in its own scratch directory, because two processes
must not write one `out_dir`. On a pilot-scale submission that is not 1.4s.
Sharing a read-only prepared plant across processes is possible and was not
built.

**(f) THE AUDIT ACCEPT'S CHILD CARRIES ITS LINEAGE IN THE REGISTRY, NOT ITS
DOCUMENT.** `parent_schedule_id` comes back on the response and is stored on the
schedule row; the document has no `scenario` block because the child is a base
schedule. Correct as far as it goes, and it means a document read in isolation
cannot say it came from an audit.

**(g) `det_consumed` WAS ADDED TO `RollingView` THIS SESSION** so a member row
could answer "was it the seed or the budget?". The first sequential measurement
ran before it landed and was discarded and re-run; the two runs' five ledger
totals were identical and their wall totals differed by 55 seconds.

**(h) THE PORTFOLIO IS NOT REACHABLE FROM THE ASK PATH.** Nobody can ask "how
much did the other seeds find?". §5a.29's shape (the cost proof is rendered and
voiced but there is no optimality INTENT) now has a second member.
